How to bid on federal contracts

A plain-English path from zero to your first government contract.

Step by stepSet-asides Notice typesGlossary

Step by step

  1. Register in SAM.gov (free)

    Create a Login.gov account, then register your business at SAM.gov to get a UEI and CAGE code. Registration is free and takes a few days to a few weeks. Be wary of companies charging to register you, and renew every year.

  2. Know your NAICS codes

    List every NAICS code that matches your work in your SAM profile. Agencies search by these codes, and each opportunity's code decides who can bid. Our NAICS guide lists the codes we track.

  3. Check your size standard

    Look up the SBA size standard for each of your codes. If you are under it, you qualify for small-business set-asides, which is where most new contractors win.

  4. Get certifications that fit

    8(a), HUBZone, WOSB and SDVOSB certifications open set-aside contracts with less competition. Apply through certify.sba.gov (VetCert for veteran-owned).

  5. Respond to Sources Sought notices

    These cost nothing to answer and show the agency there are capable small businesses, which can convince them to set the contract aside.

  6. Read the whole solicitation

    Read the scope (SOW/PWS), instructions (Section L), evaluation criteria (Section M), wage determinations, bonding and insurance requirements, and every amendment. Note the questions deadline and any site visit.

  7. Price it carefully

    Include required wages and benefits, travel, bonds, insurance and overhead. Award notices on SAM.gov and USAspending.gov show what similar contracts went for.

  8. Submit early

    Submit exactly as instructed (email, PIEE or another portal) before the response date and time zone. Acknowledge every amendment. Late submissions are almost always rejected.

  9. Win, perform, build a record

    Do the work well. Your CPARS ratings and past performance make the next award easier. If you lose, ask for a debriefing to learn what scored low.

Registering in SAM.gov is always free. Never pay a company that says it's required to register you.
